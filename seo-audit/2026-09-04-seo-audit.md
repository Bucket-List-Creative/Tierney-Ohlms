## Executive summary

The Next.js site in this repository is in materially better technical shape than the site currently serving tierneyohlms.com, and the single most important finding is that **these are two different codebases**. The live site is a Lovable-built single-page app: a 12.7KB shell with an empty `<div id="root">`, two real routes (`/` and `/unsubscribe`), and a catch-all that returns HTTP 200 for every URL a crawler tries. The replacement is a fully server-rendered Next.js 16 site with twelve real pages, all of whose content is present in the HTML before any JavaScript runs.

That reframes the work. This is not an SEO clean-up of a live site; it is a **replatform with a cutover**, and the audit is organized around that.

The good news is substantial and worth stating plainly, because it is the part a generic audit would skip past. Rendering is not a problem — the usual React catastrophe of shipping an empty page to crawlers does not apply here. The homepage delivers 128KB of real HTML with a correct single `<h1>`, a clean heading hierarchy, and every one of the seven service pages linked. Internal linking is genuinely good. The contact form is properly labelled with `autocomplete` attributes, a honeypot, and a skip link — accessibility is above average for a marketing site. Service page copy is specific and non-generic in a way that is hard to buy.

The problems cluster in two places. First, **machine-readable identity is entirely absent**: no canonical tags on any of the thirteen built pages, no `sitemap.xml`, no `robots.txt`, and zero structured data. The incumbent has all four. Launching as-is would be a visible regression in exactly the signals that matter for a local professional-services firm — which makes this the one thing that genuinely blocks cutover. Second, there is **drift between the Sanity CMS and the components that are supposed to render it**: five of seven homepage sections hardcode their `<h2>` and ignore the editable heading field, one section renders a duplicate card and silently drops two others, and an unsubstantiated "60% typical savings" claim is baked into a component where no editor can reach it.

Two decisions need the business, not the developer. The **"60% savings" claim** needs a real basis or it needs to come out — for an accounting firm, an unsourced savings figure is a trust and compliance question before it is an SEO one. And the **Privacy Policy and Terms of Service links both point at `#top`**, which for a firm handling client financial data is a gap that a prospect will notice.

Nothing here requires rearchitecting. The foundation is sound; what is missing is a metadata and structured-data layer that does not yet exist, and a short list of content corrections.

## Website classification

**Primary type:** Professional services — accounting/CPA firm.
**Secondary type:** Local service business, single location, with a B2B lead-generation conversion model.

**Evidence:** A real downtown St. Louis office (1015 Locust Street, Suite 1000, Saint Louis, MO 63101) rendered from CMS data, a published phone number and email, two named CPA founders with biographies and photographs, seven distinct service offerings arranged as a ladder from catch-up bookkeeping to controller oversight, a flat-monthly-fee positioning, and a conversion path built on a contact form plus a `tel:` link. A Financial-Cents client portal link confirms an ongoing-engagement model rather than transactional sales.

**What this classification means for strategy:**

- `LocalBusiness` markup is core identity here, not decoration. Google's documentation directs using the most specific accurate subtype; `AccountingService` is more precise than the `ProfessionalService` the incumbent currently uses, and both are valid `LocalBusiness` subtypes.
- **Authorship genuinely matters.** Professional services and finance carry a higher evidence and accuracy bar. The two named CPAs are a real asset, and `Person` markup on them is legitimate — this is one of the few site types where that is true.
- Google Business Profile alignment is central, not optional.

**What is deliberately NOT being recommended, and why:**

- **No city or location pages.** One real office, one metro. Generating "outsourced accounting in [suburb]" pages is the single most common way a firm like this trips Google's doorway-page policies. If the client confirms genuine multi-county operations with distinct content, one well-built service-area page is the ceiling — not thirty templated ones.
- **No FAQPage structured data.** See the research table: Google restricted FAQ rich results to government and health sites in September 2023 and deprecated the feature entirely in May 2026. The FAQ *content* on the homepage is good and should stay; the markup would produce nothing.
- **No `AggregateRating` or `Review` markup.** Google's LocalBusiness documentation states these properties are "only recommended for sites that capture reviews about other local businesses." A firm marking up its own rating is self-serving review markup. Real reviews displayed on the page remain valuable to users regardless.
- **No blog, yet.** Seven service pages that convert beat forty articles that do not. Revisit once the service pages are complete and measurable.
- **No `llms.txt`.** See P3.

## P0 — Critical (blocks cutover)

### P0-1 — Structured data would regress at launch

**Category:** Structured data / Local
**Affected:** All routes; no schema layer exists in the repo.

**Issue:** The new site emits zero JSON-LD. The incumbent emits a `ProfessionalService` block containing name, description, URL, telephone, email, a full `PostalAddress`, `geo` coordinates, `areaServed`, and `priceRange`.

**Evidence:** `audit_html.py` across all 13 built files reports `no structured data: 13`. `curl` of the live homepage returns one valid `application/ld+json` block.

**Why it matters:** Structured data does not cause rankings — it can make a page eligible for rich results and it is how a search engine reliably resolves *which business this is*. For a single-location professional-services firm, that identity signal is core. Shipping a replacement that removes it trades a working signal for nothing. (Tier: **Confirmed** — Google documents LocalBusiness support and the knowledge-panel/carousel outcomes.)

**Proposed fix:** Build an `Organization` + `LocalBusiness` graph, using `AccountingService` as the most specific accurate subtype, generated from Sanity `siteSettings` rather than hardcoded. Add `BreadcrumbList` on service pages and `Person` for the two founders. Carry across everything the incumbent already asserts — all of it is verifiable against page content. Omit any property without real data rather than emitting empty strings.

**Expected impact:** Restores parity with the current site and makes the business eligible for knowledge-panel treatment it may already receive. No ranking promise.
**Effort:** Medium · **Risk:** Low — additive, no visual change.

**Shipped in commit `38a4e4d`**, with the NAP and geo fields that feed it made required in `siteSettings` (commit below) so the graph cannot be hollowed out by an editor clearing a field. See **P1-9** for the underlying merge issue that validation only partially contains.

### P0-2 — No canonical tags anywhere, and both hosts serve 200

**Category:** Technical
**Affected:** All 13 built pages; `app/layout.tsx`.

**Issue:** Not one page emits `<link rel="canonical">`. Separately, `https://tierneyohlms.com/` and `https://www.tierneyohlms.com/` both return HTTP 200 with no redirect between them.

**Evidence:** `missing canonical: 13` across the built output. `curl -sI` returns 200 for both hosts.

**Why it matters:** With the confirmed decision to canonicalize on `www`, the site needs both a 301 from apex and self-referencing canonicals. Without them, two hosts serving identical content leaves consolidation to Google's discretion. (Tier: **Best practice**, consistent with Google's canonicalization guidance.)

**Proposed fix:** Emit `alternates.canonical` per route, derived from the route path rather than typed by editors. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL=https://www.tierneyohlms.com` in the Vercel environment — it is currently absent from `.env.local`, so `metadataBase` silently falls back to the apex host in `app/layout.tsx:6`. Add the apex → www 301 at the platform level.

**Expected impact:** Removes host ambiguity before it can be indexed.
**Effort:** Low · **Risk:** Low — but the env var must be set *before* first deploy, or canonicals will point at the wrong host.

### P0-3 — No `robots.txt` and no `sitemap.xml`

**Category:** Technical
**Affected:** `app/` — neither file nor route handler exists.

**Issue:** Neither artifact exists in the repo or the build. The incumbent serves both.

**Evidence:** `find` across `app/`, `public/`, and `lib/` returns no robots or sitemap file; `out/` contains neither.

**Why it matters:** A sitemap is not required for a twelve-page site to be discovered, but it is how you declare the canonical URL set and supply honest `lastmod` values — and it is the fastest way to get a new URL set crawled after a replatform. `robots.txt` is where the sitemap gets referenced.

**Proposed fix:** Add `app/sitemap.ts` returning `MetadataRoute.Sitemap`, generated from the Sanity service and page slugs so it cannot drift, with `lastModified` sourced from each document's `_updatedAt` (currently not queried — see P2-6). Add `app/robots.ts` referencing the sitemap. Both are confirmed conventions in Next.js 16.3.

**Expected impact:** Faster, more complete discovery of the new URL set at cutover.
**Effort:** Low · **Risk:** None.

## P1 — High impact

### P1-1 — Title template double-brands every CMS-driven page

**Category:** Content
**Affected:** `app/layout.tsx:9-12`; homepage and About titles.

**Issue:** The root template appends `· Tierney & Ohlms`, but the Sanity `metaTitle` values already include the firm name. Results: `Tierney & Ohlms | Bookkeeping & Controller Services · Tierney & Ohlms` (69 characters) and `Our Story | Tierney & Ohlms · Tierney & Ohlms`.

**Evidence:** Extracted from built HTML; lengths measured by `audit_html.py`.

**Why it matters:** The brand consumes the title twice, pushing descriptive terms toward truncation and wasting the most valuable line in the result. (Tier: **Best practice** — Google may rewrite titles regardless, which is normal and not a defect to chase.)

**Proposed fix:** Decide one owner of the brand suffix. Cleanest is to strip the brand from the Sanity values and let the template append it, so editors never have to remember. Add a `max(60)` **warning** (not error) on `seo.metaTitle`.

**Effort:** Low · **Risk:** Low — content-only, needs a pass over existing CMS values.

### P1-2 — `ogImage` is a live editor trap; no social image is ever emitted

**Category:** Content / Sanity
**Affected:** `lib/sanity/queries.ts` (all five projections); every `generateMetadata`.

**Issue:** `ogImage` exists in the `seo` schema and is fetched — with full URL, LQIP, dimensions and alt — in every GROQ projection. **No `generateMetadata` reads it.** No page emits `og:image`, and `twitter:card` therefore defaults to `summary`.

**Evidence:** `grep` for `openGraph` in the source returns only `app/layout.tsx:15`. Zero `og:image` tags across 13 built pages.

**Why it matters:** Two costs. Every share of this site on LinkedIn, Slack, or in a message renders without an image. And an editor can upload a social image in the Studio, see it saved, and never learn it does nothing. (Tier: **UX / conversion** for the image itself; the orphaned field is a maintainability defect.)

**Proposed fix:** Wire the fallback chain in the templates — `seo.ogImage` → a site-wide default from `siteSettings` → nothing — and add `twitter.card: "summary_large_image"` once an image exists. Add a default OG image field to `siteSettings`.

**Effort:** Low · **Risk:** None.

### P1-3 — Homepage renders a duplicate card and silently drops two others

**Category:** Content
**Affected:** `components/sections/WhyUs.tsx`.

**Issue:** The "Cost-Effective" card is hardcoded in the component *and* is the first item returned from Sanity, so it renders twice — two identical `<h3>Cost-Effective</h3>` on the homepage. Meanwhile `features.slice(0,4)` truncates the six-item feature list, so `Dedicated Support` and `Peace of Mind` never render from the CMS (the latter is separately hardcoded as the dark banner).

**Evidence:** Heading extraction from `out/index.html` shows `Cost-Effective` twice. `scripts/seed.ndjson` contains six `feature` documents; four appear.

**Why it matters:** A duplicated heading is a visible content defect. More seriously, the Features document type behaves unpredictably — adding a seventh feature does nothing, and reordering leaves the hardcoded duplicate in place.

**Proposed fix:** Remove the hardcoded first card and render the full list from Sanity, or drop the duplicate from the CMS and document that the first card is intentionally fixed. The first is preferable — it restores the CMS as the source of truth.

**Effort:** Low · **Risk:** Low — visual layout must be preserved; the first card has a distinct wide treatment.

### P1-5 — Five of seven homepage sections ignore their Sanity heading field

**Category:** Sanity architecture
**Affected:** `Services.tsx`, `WhyUs.tsx`, `Process.tsx`, `Faq.tsx`, `Contact.tsx`.

**Issue:** Each reads `eyebrow` and `lead` from the CMS but hardcodes its `<h2>`. The `sectionHeader.heading` field is marked `required` in the schema, is presented as editable in the Studio, and has no effect on the page. Only `Hero` and `CtaBanner` honour it.

**Evidence:** `grep` for `header.heading` returns 0 matches in those five files, each of which contains a literal `<h2>`.

**Why it matters:** Headings are among the strongest on-page signals, and here they cannot be changed without a deploy. The Studio actively misleads the editor about what it controls.

**Proposed fix:** Render `header.heading` in all five, preserving the existing emphasis treatment (the `<em class="gradient-text">` on the closing phrase can be derived, as `ServiceLadder` already does with `LastWordEmphasis`). Alternatively, remove the field — but that discards editorial control the schema promises.

**Effort:** Medium · **Risk:** Medium — the emphasis styling is the fiddly part. Visual output must be identical.

### P1-9 — `getHomeData` falls back per document, not per field

**Priority:** P1 — post-launch
**Category:** Sanity architecture
**Affected:** `lib/data.ts:54-70`

**Issue:** The fallback to the local content mirror fires only when a Sanity document is absent entirely (`site ?? fb.site`). Once a document exists, GROQ returns `null` for every field it lacks, and `{...fb.home, ...home}` lets those nulls overwrite the mirror's values.

**Evidence:** Verified two ways on 2026-09-04. A GROQ projection over a partial object returns explicit `null`s for absent fields — confirmed against the live API. And running the real schema builder against a `siteSettings` document with only `wordmark` populated produces an `AccountingService` node reduced to `@type`, `@id`, `name`, `url`, `parentOrganization` — `address` and `geo` gone. The same merge blanks `homePage.seo` when the document exists but the SEO group is untouched.

**Why it matters:** Two distinct failure modes. Partial seeding, which disappears once the dataset is filled — and **an editor clearing a field**, which does not, and which becomes the *only* behaviour once every document exists and the fallback is permanently off. Both are silent: valid markup, successful build, quietly diminished structured data. (Tier: **Best practice** — this is a correctness and editor-safety issue, not a documented search-engine requirement.)

**Proposed fix:** Make the merge genuinely per-field — deep-merge the mirror under the Sanity response, discarding `null` and `undefined` from the CMS side rather than letting them win. A small `mergeDefined(fallback, incoming)` helper applied to `site`, `nav` and `home` covers it. Then correct or delete the comment on line 54.

**Why not now:** It changes the resolution path for every piece of content on the site, which wants its own verification pass against a seeded dataset. Required-field validation on `siteSettings` (shipped this pass) blocks the highest-consequence version — a published document with no NAP or geo — in the meantime.

**Expected impact:** No search-visibility change on its own. Prevents a class of silent regression.
**Effort:** Low–Medium · **Risk:** Medium — touches how all content resolves; needs testing against real CMS data, not the mirror.

## P2 — Medium

**P2-1 — Heading level skip on `/services`.** The seven service cards render as `<h3>` directly under the `<h1>` with no intervening `<h2>`, on what is arguably the most commercially important page. Fix by promoting to `<h2>` or introducing a section heading. *Change the tag, keep the visual style.*

**P2-2 — `BreadcrumbList` on service pages.** Real hierarchy exists (`Home › Services › [Service]`). Confirmed-supported, desktop, and visible breadcrumbs are not required — though adding them would also help users orient. Low effort.

**P2-3 — `Person` markup for the two founders.** Real named CPAs with credentials, biographies, and photographs already on `/about`. One of the few site types where this is genuinely warranted.

**P2-4 — `siteSettings` cannot produce clean structured data.** `addressLine1` / `addressLine2` are free-text strings and `hours` is a single string, so neither decomposes into `PostalAddress` or `openingHoursSpecification` without parsing. Missing entirely: legal name, logo, social profile URLs (`sameAs`), geo coordinates, service area, Google Business Profile URL. Restructuring this is a prerequisite for doing P0-1 properly rather than with hardcoded values.

**P2-5 — `seo` object is missing two useful fields.** No `noIndex` toggle and no `canonicalOverride`. Neither is urgent at twelve pages, but both become necessary as the `page` type gets used. Also: `ogImage` has no `alt` field, unlike the hero and founder images which correctly require one.

**P2-6 — `_updatedAt` is never queried.** No projection fetches it, so sitemap `lastmod` would have to come from build time — which is noise. Add it to the service and page projections when building the sitemap.

**P2-7 — Oversized source images.** `public/img/Dan Tierney.jpg` is 1.7MB and `assets/map.png` is 812KB. On Vercel these pass through `next/image` and will be optimized, so the practical impact is small — but the source assets should be compressed, and the filenames contain spaces (URL-encoded as `%20`), which is worth fixing at upload. *Note: the audit script flagged "images without dimensions" on these; that is a false positive — they use `next/image` with `fill`, and the parent reserves space, so there is no layout-shift risk.*

**P2-8 — Root-level `page` slugs can collide with static routes.** `page` documents resolve at `/[slug]` while `/about` and `/services` are hardcoded routes. A `page` slugged `about` would be silently unreachable. Add slug validation, or namespace CMS pages under a prefix.

**P2-9 — Retire the GitHub Pages build path.** With Vercel confirmed, the `NEXT_PUBLIC_STATIC_EXPORT` branch in `next.config.ts`, the `REPO_BASE_PATH` constant, and `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` — which deletes `app/api`, `app/studio`, and `app/(site)/[slug]` before building — are dead weight that will mislead the next developer. The dual-mode `ContactForm` fallback can be simplified alongside it. Removing this also eliminates the `metadataBase`/`basePath` conflict permanently.

**P2-11 — A build immediately after a content change can render stale data.** Observed during the seeding verification on 2026-09-04: the dataset was seeded, `.next` was deleted, and the very next build still produced a sitemap with **no `lastmod` entries** — because `useCdn: true` served the pre-seed (empty) response and every query fell back to the local mirror. A second build minutes later picked up all 7 dates. The build did not fail or warn; it silently produced mirror-backed output. This matters twice over: at cutover, a deploy fired straight after seeding may ship stale content, and the Sanity publish webhook that revalidates the site is exposed to the same window. Worth deciding deliberately whether the sitemap and metadata paths should use `useCdn: false` (fresh, slightly slower) or whether the deploy should simply not run within the CDN propagation window. **Note this also masked the verification:** because the seed and the mirror hold identical values, `lastmod` was the only signal distinguishing the two sources.

**P2-12 — The seed is not a faithful mirror of `lib/content`.** Seeding exposed a concrete instance of P1-9: `scripts/seed.ndjson`'s `homePage` document had no `seo` field, while the local mirror does. The moment the CMS took over, GROQ returned `seo: null`, the per-document merge let that null win, and `generateMetadata` returned `title: undefined` — which **suppresses the layout's default title rather than inheriting it**. The homepage shipped with no `<title>`, no meta description and no `og:title`, on a build that otherwise looked healthy. Fixed by adding `seo` to the seed and re-upserting the document. The general lesson: the seed and the mirror must be kept in step field-for-field, and any future divergence produces exactly this class of silent loss. Worth a check that compares the two on CI.

**P2-10 — Only the active service is a heading on the homepage.** The pinned-scroll `ServiceLadder` renders the current service as `<h3>` and the rest as spans. All seven names and all seven links *are* present in the HTML, so this is not a crawlability problem — it is a minor semantic one. Low priority; do not restructure a working interaction for it.

## P3 — Experimental / emerging

Kept separate so nothing here is mistaken for the plan. All checked 2026-09-04.

**1. `llms.txt` — recommendation: skip.**
Tier: **Confirmed (against)**. Google's documentation states llms.txt has no effect on Search or AI Overviews and that Search ignores the file, and Google's AI-features guidance says explicitly: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features." Independent analysis of 94,000+ cited URLs found no measurable citation uplift. This is unusually well-settled for this space. Revisit only if a major platform documents consumption.

**2. `Google-Extended` — a business decision, not a technical default.**
Tier: **Confirmed** (mechanism) / business judgement (the choice). Blocking it prevents use in Gemini training and grounding and has **no effect on Google Search rankings or inclusion**. A common misconception worth correcting: it does **not** control AI Overviews, which draw on the standard Googlebot-crawled index — you cannot opt out of AI Overviews while remaining in Search. For a firm that would benefit from being recommended by assistants, allowing it is the likelier preference, but this is the client's call and should be asked rather than assumed. No directive should be added unilaterally.

**3. Entity consistency across external profiles.**
Tier: **Hypothesis** (plausible, undocumented). ### Content-source resolution — and why partial seeding is risky

Verified empirically on 2026-09-04, because the behaviour is not obvious from reading the code.

`isSanityConfigured` is **true** (a real project ID is set), so the app *does* query Sanity on every request. The queries simply return nothing today, and each falls back differently:

| Consumer | Reads Sanity? | Fallback | Granularity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Canonical tags** | No — derived from the route path | n/a | n/a. The *value* never depends on CMS data. |
| **Which pages exist** | Yes — `generateStaticParams` | Services fall back to the mirror; CMS `page` documents do not (none exist → no routes) | Per collection |
| **JSON-LD** | Yes — `siteSettings` via `getHomeData` | `site ?? fb.site` | **Whole document** |
| **Sitemap** | Yes — `sitemapEntriesQuery` | Services fall back to the mirror; pages do not | Per collection |

Two verified facts drive the risk:

1. **A GROQ projection returns `null` for fields the document lacks** — confirmed against the live API. A half-filled document is therefore a *truthy object full of nulls*, not a partial one.
2. **`site ?? fb.site` only fires when the document is absent entirely.** Once any `siteSettings` document exists, the mirror is bypassed completely.

Running the real schema builder against a `siteSettings` document with only `wordmark` filled produces:

```
FULL (mirror today)   Organization: @type @id name url description email telephone address
                      AccountingService: … address, geo, parentOrganization
PARTIAL (one field)   Organization: @type @id name url
                      AccountingService: @type @id name url parentOrganization   ← address and geo gone
```

The markup does not break — the builder omits missing properties by design rather than emitting empty strings — but it silently hollows out. The same applies to the homepage: creating the `homePage` document without filling `seo` blanks the meta title and description, because `{...fb.home, ...home}` lets GROQ's nulls win. Only `hero` has genuine sub-field protection, and only while `hero` itself is null.

#### The scenario that outlives seeding: an editor clearing a field

Partial seeding is the version of this problem that goes away once the dataset is filled. **The editor-blanking scenario does not.**

Once the dataset is seeded, every document exists — so the `?? fb.site` fallback is permanently switched off. From that point on, an editor who clears a field in the Studio is not falling back to the mirror; they are publishing an empty value. Concretely:

- Clearing **`geo`** or any part of **`address`** silently strips `PostalAddress` and `GeoCoordinates` out of the `AccountingService` block on every page. The markup stays valid, so nothing errors and no build fails — the business identity just quietly stops being machine-readable.
- Clearing **`seo.metaDescription`** on the homepage removes the meta description sitewide rather than reverting to the mirror's copy.
- Clearing **`phoneHref`** removes `telephone` from both the `Organization` and `AccountingService` nodes.

This is a normal thing for an editor to do — emptying a field to "reset it" is a reasonable mental model, and nothing in the Studio previously suggested otherwise. It is also invisible: the page still renders, the site still builds, and the loss only shows up in a structured-data test weeks later.

**Mitigated in this pass** by making the fields that hollow out the graph required in `siteSettings` — `phone`, `phoneHref`, `email`, both display address lines, every part of the structured `address`, and both `geo` coordinates. Sanity surfaces these as validation errors and **blocks publishing** the document; note that drafts still autosave in the invalid state, so this is a publish gate, not a save gate. That is the appropriate guardrail — it stops the bad state reaching production without fighting the editor mid-edit.

Validation guards `siteSettings`. It does not fix the underlying merge, which still lets GROQ nulls overwrite mirror values across `homePage` — hence the P1 below.

The comment on `lib/data.ts:54` — *"Fall back per-field so a not-yet-seeded document never blanks the page"* — describes an intent the code does not implement.
